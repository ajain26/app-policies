Kidzy Learn for Android

Privacy Policy

Effective date: October 20, 2026

The short version: Kidzy Learn is free and shows ads from Google AdMob that are set up for children. Every ad request is marked as child-directed, so Google does not show personalized ads or remarketing, ads are limited to content for general audiences, and the app has no permission to read Android's advertising ID. There is no account, no server of ours and no analytics. Your child's name, avatar, age level and progress stay on the device and are never given to the ads. Google's ad code does send Google some technical information in order to show ads, and this page lists it.

Who we are

Kidzy Learn, listed on Google Play as "Kidzy: ABC Phonics & Tracing", is an Android learning app for young children, roughly ages 2 to 7, with nine activities covering the alphabet, phonics, numbers, tracing, spelling, colors, shapes, quizzes and puzzles. It is published on Google Play by Anshul Apps ("we", "us"). This policy explains what the Android app handles and how. It is written to be read by a parent.

Children's privacy

Kidzy Learn is made for children, so this section comes first and it is the most important part of this page.

Kidzy Learn itself collects no personal information from your child, and none from you. There is no account, no sign in, no server of ours, and nothing in the app that sends your child's details anywhere. The one thing that goes over the internet on its own is advertising: Google's ad code, which shows the ads that keep Kidzy free, sends Google the technical information listed under Ads below. Point by point:

  • Ads are set up for children. Every ad request Kidzy makes is tagged for child-directed treatment, the setting Google provides for apps covered by the United States Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA). For such requests Google takes steps to turn off interest-based (personalized) advertising and remarketing. Ads are limited to content rated for general audiences (G).
  • No advertising ID. Android's advertising ID permission is removed from the app, Kidzy never reads the advertising ID, and every ad request is tagged child-directed, which Google says prevents Google's ad code from sending it.
  • Nothing about your child goes to the ads. Kidzy never gives Google's ad code your child's name, avatar, age level or progress.
  • No analytics and no trackers of our own. There is no analytics SDK, no crash reporting service and no attribution or marketing SDK. Apart from Google's ad code, nothing in the app sends data anywhere on its own, and the activities work in airplane mode.
  • Almost nothing is asked for. There is no email field, no birthday, no photograph, no location, no contacts and no sign in. The only free text anywhere in the app is a child's first name on a profile, and a nickname or a first initial does that job just as well.
  • Nothing a child can send. There is no chat, no messaging, no camera, no photo or file access, no comments, no other users to reach and no web browsing inside the app.
  • A grown-up gate in front of everything that leaves the app, except ads. Opening the support, privacy or terms pages (at the foot of the progress screen), sharing a progress card, printing a tracing worksheet, turning the daily reminder on, and renaming or deleting a child's profile all require solving a math question first. The Google Play rating prompt is only ever offered after a grown-up has passed that gate. Ads are the exception: an ad is shown by Google's code, not ours, and tapping one can open the advertiser's web page or Google Play outside Kidzy, so the gate cannot stand in front of it. There are two kinds: a banner at the bottom of the home screen, and now and then a full-screen ad when your child leaves an activity after finishing a round, before the home screen comes back. Ads never interrupt an activity, and a full-screen ad never appears when the app opens or over the home screen.

Some things sit outside the gate on purpose, because they reveal nothing: switching between children, choosing "Maybe Later" on the reminder explainer, and turning the reminder off. The gate is a sensible barrier rather than a security system, so pair it with Google Family Link if you have an older child who enjoys a challenge.

About COPPA. COPPA governs the personal information collected online from children under 13. We operate no server, and nothing your child types or does in Kidzy Learn is sent to us, so we hold no data about your child for a parent to review, export or delete. The technical information Google's ad code sends is collected by Google from ad requests that Kidzy marks as child-directed, which is how Google is told to apply its COPPA treatment to them. Google describes how it handles that information in its own policies, linked under Ads below.

Children in Europe and the United Kingdom. Kidzy never asks a child, or anyone else, to agree to personalized ads, because it never requests them. Google's documentation says that ad requests from the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland that carry no consent signal are answered, where possible, with "limited ads", which do not use personal data, or an identifier stored on the device, to personalize ads; otherwise no ad is shown. As everywhere else, Kidzy keeps your child's information on the device and never sends it anywhere on its own.

We do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone of any age.

Ads

Kidzy Learn is free, with every activity open, and it is supported by ads served by Google AdMob, a service of Google LLC. This is how they are set up:

  • Where they appear. A banner at the bottom of the home screen, the screen where your child picks an activity. It sits in a space kept free for it, so nothing on the screen moves when an ad loads. Now and then a full-screen ad, only when your child leaves an activity after finishing a round, before the home screen comes back. Ads never interrupt an activity. A full-screen ad never appears when the app opens or within three minutes of it opening, never more than once in three minutes, never over the home screen or an open pop-up, and never within a minute of the grown-up gate.
  • What they are. Text and image ads only, some of them animated or interactive, and no video ads. A full-screen ad has its own close button, which Google's ad code shows after a short countdown, within about 5 seconds.
  • Child-directed. Every request is tagged for child-directed treatment, so Google turns off personalized ads and remarketing for it.
  • Content. Limited to content rated G (general audiences), both in the app's ad requests and in our AdMob settings, which also block sensitive ad categories, including alcohol and gambling. If you ever see an ad that does not belong in an app for children, please tell us at the address below.

To show, measure and limit ads and to prevent fraud, the Google Mobile Ads SDK in the app collects the following and shares it with Google, according to Google's own disclosure for this SDK:

  • the device's IP address, which can be used to estimate the general location of the device
  • basic device and app information that ad requests carry, such as the device model, the Android version, the language and the app's version
  • how the app and its ads are used, such as app launches, ad views and taps on ads
  • diagnostic information about the performance of the app and of the ads SDK, such as app launch time, hang rate and energy usage
  • device identifiers other than the advertising ID: Google lists the app set ID, an identifier Android gives to the apps of one developer on a device, and, where it applies, identifiers related to accounts signed in on the device

Google states that this data is encrypted in transit. The advertising ID is not collected: the app does not have Android's advertising ID permission, and Google says child-directed requests do not send it. We receive only aggregated reports from AdMob, such as how many ads were shown, which do not identify your child. Without an internet connection no new ad can load, and every activity keeps working; an ad that loaded earlier, while the device was online, can still be shown. How Google uses information from apps that show its ads is described at policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites, and in the Google Privacy Policy.

What the app keeps, and where

Everything Kidzy Learn saves is written to the app's own private storage on your device, which other apps cannot read. This is what it keeps:

  • Profiles. For each child, a first name, an avatar chosen from eight animal emoji, and an age level of 2 to 3, 4 to 5, or 6 to 7. Up to four children can each have a profile.
  • Learning progress. Which letters, numbers, colors, shapes and sounds have been explored, spelling and quiz results, tracing and puzzle completion, and a count of activities finished on each day.
  • Streaks and badges. The current and longest daily streak, the last day the app was used, the total number of active days, and which badges have been earned.
  • App state. Whether the introduction has been seen, whether the daily reminder is on, and a small record of how often the app has offered the Google Play rating prompt.
  • Housekeeping. A few values the app needs to behave itself: how many times the math question has just been missed and when the short pause after three misses runs out, whether the reminder explainer has already been shown, and the identifier Android hands back for the reminder it has scheduled, so the app can cancel that reminder later.

Each profile is given an identifier made on the device from the moment it was created plus a random number. It is not a device identifier, it is not linked to you, and it means nothing outside that one device. The app sends none of the information above anywhere on its own, and none of it is given to the ads. Google's ad code also keeps a few technical values of its own in the app's storage, such as its settings and cached ad data.

Your child's name

Setting the app up asks for a first name, and so does adding a second, third or fourth child. It is the only personal detail the app has a field for. A nickname or a first initial works just as well, and is what we would suggest.

A name is trimmed to twenty characters when it is saved. It can be changed later from the profile switcher, after the grown-up gate, but the box will not save a blank one, so a name can be replaced rather than cleared. If you would rather no name were saved at all, the introduction has a Skip button in the top corner of every screen before the last one, and using it finishes setup without asking for a name. The home screen then greets your child with a wave instead of a name, and the weekly progress card says "your little learner".

When a name is saved, it is used in three places, all of them on your own device:

  • On screen, to greet your child and to label their progress.
  • In the spelling activity, where the name joins the list of words to spell if it is exactly three, four or five letters long, matching the word lengths of the Easy, Medium and Hard rounds. Names of any other length are left out. Most words have a recorded clip built into the app. Anything without one, including a name, is spoken by the text-to-speech engine that is part of Android on your device (on most phones, Speech Services by Google), under that engine's own privacy terms. Kidzy Learn hands the word only to that engine, asks it for a US English voice installed on the device, and makes no network request of its own to speak it.
  • On the weekly progress card, which shows the name and avatar. That card is exactly why sharing it is placed behind the grown-up gate: once you send it somewhere, it is wherever you sent it.

What we do not do

  • We do not offer or require accounts, logins or registration.
  • We do not use analytics, crash reporting or attribution SDKs.
  • We do not request personalized ads or remarketing, and the app does not read the advertising ID or have the permission to.
  • We do not send your child's name, avatar, age level or progress to any server, and we do not give them to the ads. There is no server of ours.
  • We do not sell or rent personal information.
  • We do not use anything about your child to train any model.

Purchases

This version of Kidzy Learn has no in-app purchases and no subscription, so the app handles no payment information. If a later version offers something to buy, this policy will be updated before that version is released.

Permissions

Kidzy Learn asks for one permission on screen, notifications, and only after a grown-up has passed the gate to turn the daily reminder on. On Android 12 and older there is no such prompt, and the reminder simply follows your notification settings.

Android also lists a few permissions that are granted when the app is installed and never ask anything. Each one is there for a single job:

  • Internet and network state. Used by Google's ad code to load ads. Kidzy Learn itself contacts no server of ours, because there is none.
  • Run at startup. So a daily reminder that is switched on is scheduled again after the device restarts.
  • Prevent the phone from sleeping. This comes with Google libraries the app includes. Kidzy itself never keeps the phone awake.
  • Control vibration and change audio settings. For the light taps while tracing, and for playing the app's own sounds.
  • Receive data from the internet. This comes with Google's push messaging code, which the notification library the app uses includes. It is never switched on: the app has no push configuration, never registers for push messages and creates no push token, so nothing can be received and we have no way to send you a message.

Not requested: microphone, camera, photos and files, location, contacts, calendar, phone, Bluetooth, exact alarms and the advertising ID. Kidzy Learn plays sound, it does not record it, and the microphone permission that the audio library would normally add is removed from the app.

Notifications

Reminders are off until a grown-up turns them on, and nothing can be scheduled while Android's notification permission is denied, which you are free to do when Android asks. Two places offer them, and on Android both ask the grown-up gate first: the Daily reminder switch on the progress screen, and a short explainer that can appear on the home screen once a streak has started, offering "Turn On Reminders" or "Maybe Later". Turning reminders off is a single tap and is never gated. If notifications were declined, a button opens Kidzy Learn's notification settings in Android Settings.

When they are on there is exactly one: a local notification your own device shows once a day around 5 in the afternoon (Android may deliver it a little later to save battery), saying that Pandy misses your child and that a few minutes of play keeps the streak alive. It appears in the notification category "Daily learning reminder". The wording never changes, so it contains no name, no progress and no personal information at all.

There is no push server and no push token is ever created. Turning the reminder off in the app cancels it straight away, and Android Settings can switch notifications off for the app entirely (Settings, Apps, Kidzy Learn, Notifications).

Sharing and printing

Two things can leave the app at a grown-up's request, both only after the grown-up gate, and both hand over to standard Android screens where you decide what happens next.

  • The weekly progress card is an image showing your child's name, avatar and progress. Tapping share raises the grown-up gate first, then hands the image to the Android share sheet, where you alone decide where it goes.
  • The tracing worksheet goes to the Android print screen, where you can pick a printer or save a PDF. The page itself carries no name, no avatar and no scores. It is still behind the gate on Android, because the print screen can open Google Play to add a printer service. The six letters on it are picked from the ones your child has not opened yet, but nothing about that appears on the page.

Backups

Because everything lives on the device, it can be included in the ordinary backups you may already be making. If Android backup to your Google account is switched on, or you copy apps to a new phone when setting it up, the app's stored data (profiles, progress, badges and settings) may be included, which lets a child keep their progress on a new device. Backups are handled by Google under the Google Privacy Policy, and you manage them in your device's backup settings and your Google account. We have no access to your backups.

Deleting everything

There is no server-side copy to ask us for, because there is no server. A grown-up can delete a child's profile from the profile switcher after passing the gate, as long as another profile remains. To remove everything Kidzy Learn stored on the device (profiles, progress, streaks, badges and settings), clear its storage in Android Settings (under Apps, Kidzy Learn, Storage; the exact names vary by phone) or uninstall the app. If you use Android backup, a copy may remain in your Google account's device backup until that backup is replaced or deleted, which you control there. Nothing needs to be requested from us. For information that Google's ad code sent to Google, the Google Privacy Policy explains the choices Google offers.
